The story

Ustaz Aarif Raziff went to Vietnam with his wife Ustazah Nasibah Hamzah, their son, his mother-in-law and their helper. None of their prayer apps worked for them there, and the whole family turned to him to find out when to pray. Back in Singapore, his parents had the same trouble with apps that are hard to use, full of ads, and play the azan out loud on a crowded MRT train.

So he and his wife decided to build one. Prayer Times Asia is fully focused on salah. It follows the local religious authority in each country, uses official timetables where they exist, works offline and keeps your exact location on your phone.

It is built fisabilillah, in the hope that it becomes sadaqah jariah. Their company, Nasar Digital (Nasar Pte Ltd), pays the running costs, and the app will stay free, with no outside ads and its code open for anyone to check.
