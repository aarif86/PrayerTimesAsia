Our story

It started with a trip to Vietnam.

1

He landed with no proper prayer app

Ustaz Aarif arrived in Vietnam and looked for a prayer app he could trust. The big ones show ads around your salah, charge a subscription for things that used to be free, and some have been caught sharing where their users go. None of them felt right.

2

So he asked, why not build one?

Not a startup, and not a product to sell later. A prayer app built fisabilillah, as sadaqah jariah, that knows how each country works out its times, from Singapore and Malaysia to Vietnam and Japan.

3

And promised to keep it that way

Free forever, no ads, no account, your data stays yours, and the code open for anyone to check. Read the four promises.

How it's made

One ustaz. An AI team. Every time checked.

What used to take a company of developers, one person can now build with AI. The AI writes the code. Ustaz Aarif decides what is right, and the official sources have the final word on every time.

Ustaz Aarif decides

What the app does, how it speaks, and what is right for the ummah. Nothing reaches your phone without his say.

The AI team builds

AI assistants write and test the iPhone, Android and web apps and the masjid screen, so one person can build what used to need a whole team.

The sources check

MUIS and JAKIM timetables are used as they are. Worked-out times are tested against the official tables for every day of the year, and the falak readings are with a falak committee member for review.

Travel

This is the problem that started it all, on a trip to Vietnam.

Most prayer apps use one setting for everyone. Prayer Times Asia knows how each country works out its prayer times and switches the moment you arrive, with a short welcome card that tells you what changed.

Questions

Good to know

Is it really free?

Yes. Prayer Times Asia is built fisabilillah by Ustaz Aarif Raziff and Nasar Pte Ltd. There are no third-party ads, no subscriptions and no in-app purchases.

Could the app be sold one day?

No. It is not for sale, and it will never carry ads or sell data. The code is open under a licence that keeps every copy open, and only the official app may use the Prayer Times Asia name. Masjids, organisations and well-wishers who want to help can sponsor running costs or work with us. Write to salam@prayertimes.asia.

An app built with AI? Can I trust the times?

The AI writes the code. It does not decide the times. In Singapore and Malaysia the times come straight from MUIS and JAKIM. Everywhere else they follow each country's own method, tested against the official tables for every day of the year. Ustaz Aarif decides what goes in the app, the falak readings are being reviewed by a falak committee member, and the code is open so anyone can check it.

How accurate are the times?

In Singapore and Malaysia the app uses the official MUIS and JAKIM timetables. When it has to work them out itself (offline, or in other countries), it uses each country's own method, checked against the official tables for every day of the year and set to err on the safe side, never before the prayer has begun.

Does it need the internet?

Only now and then. The year's timetable is kept on your phone, and everywhere else the times are worked out on the phone itself, so it keeps working on a plane, abroad or with no signal.

What happens to my location?

It stays on your phone. There is no account and no tracking across other apps. The app only asks the official sources for your year's timetable (by zone, never your exact spot). To know where people use it, the app sends anonymous counts with just the city and country, and you can turn them off in Settings. Full privacy details.

Is the musafir guidance a fatwa?

No. Jamak and qasar guidance is general information to help you plan. When unsure, follow your local religious authority.

My masjid's times are different. What should I do?

Please tell us. Send the masjid's name and a photo of its timetable to salam@prayertimes.asia, or use "Help verify times" inside the app.
