Our story

It started with a trip to Vietnam.

1

He landed with no proper prayer app

Ustaz Aarif, an Al‑Azhar graduate recognised by the Islamic Religious Council of Singapore (MUIS), arrived in Vietnam and looked for a prayer app he could trust. The big ones show ads around your salah and charge a subscription for things that used to be free. Even the best one he found wasn't made for Asia.

2

Back home, he decided to build one

Not a startup, and not a product to sell later. A prayer app fully focused on salah, built fisabilillah in the hope that it becomes sadaqah jariah, that knows how each country works out its times, from Singapore and Malaysia to Vietnam and Japan.

3

And promised to keep it that way

Free forever, no ads, no account, your data stays yours, and the code open for anyone to check. Read the four promises.

Getting the times right

Every time checked, every day of the year.

A prayer app is only as good as its times. So the official sources come first, and everything else is tested against them.

Your local religious authority first

In Singapore, Malaysia and Indonesia the app uses the MUIS, JAKIM and Kemenag timetables exactly as they are published. Elsewhere it follows the method your local religious authority uses.

Tested every day

Where the app works the times out itself, it is tested against the official tables for every day of the year and set to err on the safe side.

Open for anyone to check

We are seeking a review of the falak readings from qualified falak experts, and the code is open, so anyone can see how every time is worked out.

Questions

Good to know

Is it really free?

Yes. Prayer Times Asia is built fisabilillah by Ustaz Aarif Raziff. Nasar Pte Ltd, his company, pays the running costs, and we earn our living from other work, never from this app. There are no third-party ads, no subscriptions and no in-app purchases. The only thing you'll see is a small card for our own services, and you can turn it off.

Could the app be sold one day?

No. It is not for sale, and it will never carry ads or sell data. The code is open under a licence that keeps every copy open, and only the official app may use the Prayer Times Asia name.

How accurate are the times?

In Singapore, Malaysia and Indonesia the app uses the official MUIS, JAKIM and Kemenag timetables, and its own working is checked against them for every day of the year. In other countries it follows the local religious authority, such as Umm al‑Qura in Saudi Arabia or Diyanet in Türkiye, and where there isn't one it uses the method most masjids there follow. Every time is set to err on the safe side, never before the prayer has begun. See every country and its source.

I don't live in Asia. Will it work for me?

Yes. It was built in Asia and Asia comes first, but it now covers 143 countries. In London it uses the Moonsighting Committee method, in North America ISNA, in Makkah Umm al‑Qura and in Istanbul Diyanet. The app always shows which source it is using, and you can change it in Settings.

Does it need the internet?

Only now and then. The year's timetable is kept on your phone, and everywhere else the times are worked out on the phone itself, so it keeps working on a plane, abroad or with no signal.

What happens to my location?

It stays on your phone. There is no account and no tracking across other apps. The app only asks the official sources for your year's timetable (by zone, never your exact spot). To know where people use it, the app sends anonymous counts with just the city and country, and you can turn them off in Settings. Full privacy details.

Is the musafir guidance a fatwa?

No. It is general guidance to help you plan, not a fatwa. Shortening (qasar) and combining (jamak) prayers on a journey is a concession from Allah, mentioned in Surah an‑Nisa, verse 101. Ibn Abbas reported that the Prophet ﷺ combined Zohor with Asar and Maghrib with Isyak when travelling (Sahih al‑Bukhari). Scholars differ on the details, such as the distance and how many days, so when unsure, follow your local religious authority.

Whose Hijri date and Ramadan start does it follow?

By default, the religious authority of your home country, so MUIS in Singapore, the Keeper of the Rulers' Seal in Malaysia and Kemenag in Indonesia. Tap the Hijri date to choose another authority, and adjust the date by a day in Settings if your community differs.

My masjid's times are different. What should I do?

Your local religious authority and your masjid come first, so follow them. Then please tell us. Send the masjid's name and a photo of its timetable to salam@prayertimes.asia, or use "Help verify times" inside the app.
