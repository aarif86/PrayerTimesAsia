Our story

It started with a trip to Vietnam.

1

He landed with no proper prayer app

Ustaz Aarif studied at Al‑Azhar and is recognised by the Islamic Religious Council of Singapore (MUIS). In Vietnam he looked for a prayer app he could trust. The big ones show ads around your salah and make you pay for things that used to be free. Even the best one he found wasn't made for Asia.

2

Back home, he decided to build one

Not a business, and never something to sell later. Just a prayer app, built fisabilillah in the hope that it becomes sadaqah jariah. It knows how each country sets its prayer times, from Singapore and Malaysia to Vietnam and Japan.

3

And promised to keep it that way

Free forever. No ads, no sign up, your information stays with you, and anyone can see how it works. Read the four promises.

Getting the times right

Every prayer time checked, every day of the year.

A prayer app is only as good as its times. So we use the official times first, and check everything else against them.

Your local religious authority first

In Singapore, Malaysia and Indonesia the app shows the MUIS, JAKIM and Kemenag times exactly as they are. Everywhere else it follows your local religious authority.

Tested every day

When the app works out the times itself, we check them against the official times for every day of the year. If in doubt, it leans a little later, so you never pray too early.

Open for anyone to check

We are asking falak experts to check our work, and anyone can see exactly how each time is worked out.

Who it's for

For everyone who prays, wherever life takes them.

Prayer times

Travel

Land in a new country, the times are already right

This is the problem that started it all, on a trip to Vietnam.

Most prayer apps use one setting for everyone. Prayer Times Asia knows how each country sets its prayer times. It changes over as soon as you arrive, and shows a short note telling you what changed.

  • For travellers, it shows how far you are from home, and whether you can shorten (qasar) and combine (jamak) your prayers
  • Follows the Shafi'i or Hanafi rules on distance and days
  • Works on your phone without internet, so no roaming needed
Qibla & Falak

Face the Kaabah in one glance

A simple compass that turns green and vibrates when you face the qibla. You can also find the time and the qibla from the sun and its shadow, the traditional way, with no signal needed.

  • Direction and distance to Makkah
  • Countdown to the days when the sun is right above the Kaabah, so you can check the qibla yourself
  • Read the time from your shadow and the sun, like in the old days

Questions

Good to know

Is it really free?

Yes. Ustaz Aarif Raziff builds it fisabilillah. His company, Nasar Pte Ltd, pays the costs, and we earn our living from other work, never from this app. There are no ads from others, no subscriptions and nothing to buy. The only thing you'll see is a small card for our own services, and you can turn it off.

Could the app be sold one day?

No. It is not for sale, and it will never show ads or sell your information. Anyone can see how it is made, any copy must stay open the same way, and only the official app can use the Prayer Times Asia name.

How accurate are the times?

In Singapore, Malaysia and Indonesia the app uses the official MUIS, JAKIM and Kemenag times, and we check our own working against them every day of the year. In other countries it follows the local religious authority, like Umm al‑Qura in Saudi Arabia or Diyanet in Türkiye. Where there isn't one, it uses what most masjids there use. If in doubt, a time leans a little later, never before the prayer has started. See every country and its source.

I don't live in Asia. Will it work for me?

Yes. It was built in Asia, but it now works in 143 countries. In London it uses the Moonsighting Committee times, in North America ISNA, in Makkah Umm al‑Qura and in Istanbul Diyanet. The app always tells you whose times it is using, and you can change it in Settings.

Does it need the internet?

Only once in a while. The times are saved on your phone, so it still works on a plane, overseas or with no signal.

What happens to my location?

It stays on your phone. There is no sign up and no tracking. To get your official times, the app only shares your zone, never your exact spot. To know where people use it, the app counts users by city and country only, without knowing who you are, and you can turn this off in Settings. Full privacy details.

Is the musafir guidance a fatwa?

No. It is general guidance to help you plan, not a fatwa. Shortening (qasar) and combining (jamak) prayers when travelling is an ease given by Allah, in Surah an‑Nisa, verse 101. Ibn Abbas said the Prophet ﷺ combined Zohor with Asar, and Maghrib with Isyak, when travelling (Sahih al‑Bukhari). Scholars differ on the details, like how far and for how many days, so if unsure, follow your local religious authority.

Whose Hijri date and Ramadan start does it follow?

Your home country's religious authority, so MUIS in Singapore, the official announcement in Malaysia and Kemenag in Indonesia. Tap the Hijri date to choose another, or move it by a day in Settings if your community is different.

My masjid's times are different. What should I do?

Your local religious authority and your masjid come first, so follow them. Then please tell us. Send the masjid's name and a photo of its timetable to salam@prayertimes.asia, or use "Help verify times" inside the app.
